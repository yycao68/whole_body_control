Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our manuscript, "Interaction Dynamics: A Physical-Interaction Reasoning Layer for Humanoid Robots," for publication in IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters. This is a journal-only submission, without a conference-presentation option.

A legged robot in contact with the world must cope with two qualitatively different kinds of disturbance: a transient push, which is best absorbed by stepping toward the fall, and a sustained force, which must instead be resisted by stepping against it. These two responses are opposite in sign, yet center-of-mass kinematics alone cannot tell them apart online, since a recovered push and a standing force leave the same lateral offset. Existing interaction controllers (impedance, admittance) assume a single interaction mode is always present and regulate its magnitude; they do not address the online decision of which mode is acting.

Our manuscript addresses this decision problem directly. We estimate the external wrench from the horizontal CoM linear-momentum residual and use its magnitude and persistence to arbitrate, through a confidence gate and a persistence-weighted blend, between a predictive capture command and an integral hold. The resulting Interaction Dynamics layer sits between a frozen reinforcement-learning walking policy and the robot, biasing only the policy's velocity command, so it requires no retraining and no whole-body inverse-dynamics realization of the correction (which we show can destabilize a strong learned policy). On a simulated Unitree G1, the layer reduces single-support push falls at 280 N from 24/40 to 2/40 (McNemar p < 1e-4, with no paired trial made worse) and holds an 8 N sustained force to within 1 mm of a dedicated hold specialist, while leaving nominal walking unchanged. A six-stage validation study additionally reports an oracle ablation, sensing-robustness sweeps, and a capture-amplification failure mode we discovered and fixed during development.

We believe the manuscript is a good fit for RA-L's scope at the intersection of legged locomotion, physical human/environment interaction, and learning-based control, and that its combination of a wrench-based mode-arbitration layer with a frozen learned policy will interest the journal's readership working on both model-based and learned locomotion controllers.

This manuscript extends the fixed-base "Interaction Dynamics" framework of our earlier work (Cao and Tang, arXiv:2606.08281) to a floating-base humanoid platform; the representation, estimator, and confidence/persistence arbitration are substantially redeveloped for locomotion and are validated on a different system, and no text or results are shared between the two manuscripts. The present work has not been published or submitted elsewhere, and all authors have approved this submission.

Full implementation details (controller equations, parameters, and a per-step algorithm listing) are provided as supplementary material.
